What this prediction assumes
  • Motor: the exact 25 time–thrust pairs from A12:B36 in your original spreadsheet. Thrust is interpolated linearly between those points and is zero after 0.857 s.
  • Drag coefficient: 0.75. An estimate for an unfinished, streamlined model rocket. Shape, fins, print layer lines, and surface finish matter; PLA alone does not determine drag.
  • Air density: 1.060375672 kg/m³. Standard-atmosphere estimate at 4,900 ft (1,493.52 m), held constant during flight. Weather changes actual density.
  • Flight: vertical, from rest, in still air. The curve ends at the peak. No recovery deployment is modeled before the peak.
  • Mass: held constant at launch-ready mass. Ignoring fuel mass loss tends to underpredict height.

Method and sources

Area A = π(d/2)². Drag D = ½ρCdAv². Acceleration a = (thrust − drag)/mass − g. Mass is converted from grams to kilograms; g = 9.80665 m/s².

Fourth-order Runge–Kutta integration uses steps no larger than 0.002 s, aligned to every thrust-data time point. The rocket remains on the pad until thrust exceeds weight. The final step is refined to locate zero upward velocity. This may give a slightly different result from the spreadsheet’s numerical approximation.

This is a classroom height estimate. It does not calculate stability, launch-rod performance, or recovery behavior. Supported input range: 1–10,000 g and 0.001–1 m; the extremes are outside a typical small model rocket.
